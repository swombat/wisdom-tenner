New version (3) 25-11-2014, 20-11-2017

There are several wisdoms, a plurality of **world-views intended to guide us while we strive for earthly lives well lived**.  **Such understanding shapes the way we navigate reality and even becomes reality, by agency and life-changing conduct.** **Beyond contemplation, wisdom is a proactive way of becoming and of being. Flourishing as a better human being.**

Differing certainties about what is real, highest and good, engender final goals sometimes alien to each other. Applied, these ways to grasp our world expand into choices of **sustainable ways to achieve the human aspirations. Those** core beliefs and choices of values, sometimes dissimilar, imply specific ways and means and competences required to do well the right thing.

What I seek among this multitude is **worldly wisdom**. I also try to find the similarities inviting mutual respect of the wise and bringing together scaterred good will usually lost by mutual misunderstanding.

It seems to me that very different doctrines of wisdom, religious, philosophical or scientific-minded may meet when it comes to their application and practice among people. To contribute to the world the wise must master some competences experienced know-how and tools of **practical wisdom.**

**Worldly wisdom, human-centred and actionable as I chose to consider it, embraces more than one way of understanding the world thrust on us as a given****and also the world we create. To comprehend our world as it is, we must** **consider many kinds of knowledge, ignorance, creeds, values, interests, virtues, vices, purposes, means and limits. The practically wise consider them all, in order to aim well, to judge well, and act well, reasonably, in the interest of us, people.**

**In short, the view of worldly wisdom which I will follow in this essay is given form by a metaphor I like to use, to "make it simple" : a "temple of wisdom" to live in. This temple metaphor is respectful of the doctrines of wisdom presented by the religions and also considers the need to keep feet on ground when we want to act and live wisely.**
